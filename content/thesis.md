# TL;DR

- Soon you'll talk to one agent, and it will run all your other agents.
- We're building that agent for early AI adopters: frontier models, no lock-in, open source.
- It's always on, knows your priorities, and only pings you when it matters.
- Agents spread into everything they can touch. You want one you trust, not five.
- Permissions and security aren't solved yet. We're working on it in public.

# Who it's for

People who want the best models, no lock-in, and open source. Early adopters.

Muse, Grok and dots will be enough for most people, and Anthropic and Gemini will ship their own soon. OpenCode's Jay Air put it well:

https://x.com/jayair/status/2105834279557341382

We're not building for most people. We're building for the ones who want more.

A lot of them are engineers. Engineers still read code, so we'll keep investing in code review.

# One agent to talk to your other agents

Everything is moving to one agent. Muse, OpenAI's dots and Grok Bot all went this way.

https://x.com/maria_rcks/status/2106620125923393849

You talk to one agent, and it handles the rest.

Nobody wants to jump between sessions all day and remember which one is doing what. Our own sidebar turns into a mess fast. So the next Happy is one agent on top. Sub-agents do the work underneath, including agents running on your own machines through Happy Desktop. You talk to one agent, and it talks to the rest.

There's a second reason it has to be one. Agents spread like a virus. Give one enough access and it will find your other sessions, get into your accounts through Chrome, and scroll through your whole digital life. Right now you probably have several of them spreading into your projects, accounts and teams. You want one you know and control.

# Why not just use Muse

We tried Muse and love the experience. Its hooks into Facebook and the rest of Meta's apps will be huge. The problem is the model. Muse Spark isn't a frontier model, and next to Opus 5.5, Fable or Astra it can't get the job done.

That's the main reason to use Happy. Bring the subscriptions you already pay for. When a better model comes out, switch to it without rebuilding your setup. dots only runs OpenAI. Muse only runs Meta. Happy runs whatever you pay for.

We'll host it for you, or you can run it yourself.

# Always on

Only the agent that knows all your priorities can decide when to ping you. Today every tool pings you on its own. One agent on top knows what's actually worth interrupting you for.

It hears about problems as they happen, and it checks on the things you care about without being asked. Cheap models like Luna can now run "is everything ok? anything new?" checks all day for almost nothing.

It runs in the cloud, so if one of your devices goes offline, it keeps going. It's your lifeline to your work.

# Fewer buttons, not zero

There are still settings you can flip by hand, and the UX still has to be good. But the real work happens in the chat.

You don't click through menus. You look at what the agent shows you, and when you want something done, you click it and it goes back to the agent. Muse does this well.

Our users want more than chat text. They want real artifacts they can look at and act on. Over time those might matter as much as the chat. The chat might change too, with the agent hiding old messages and only showing what's current.

We're keeping a sidebar, even though a lot of products are dropping theirs. You still need one place to see what's going on and decide what's next.

We're building this as a new app, not on top of the old Happy code.

# What we haven't solved

Permissions and security. The agent logs into your computers and your accounts, and it has to hand access to its sub-agents to get anything done. We don't know yet how much access it should hold, how it should pass it down, or how you check what it did. We'll work on it in public, and we'll get some of it wrong.

# How it spreads

Not through enterprise sales. Meta reached out about running an internal pilot of Happy because their engineers kept asking to use it at work. That's how good tools get into big companies: someone inside pushes for it.
