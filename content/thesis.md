# TL;DR

- One agent is your representative to the AI world. Every ping and escalation goes through it.
- Sessions become tasks. They spin off more sessions, work across repos, and form a tree.
- The sidebar is the task tracker. Think Arc's vertical tabs, generated by the agent.
- Chat stops being a log of messages. It becomes a living document the agent edits in place.
- Built for early AI adopters: frontier models, no lock-in, open source.

# Who it's for

People who want the best models, no lock-in, and open source. Early adopters.

https://x.com/jayair/status/2105834279557341382

Muse, Grok and dots will be enough for most people, and Anthropic and Gemini will ship their own soon. We're not building for most people. We're building for the ones who want more.

A lot of them are engineers. Engineers still read code, so we'll keep investing in code review.

# One agent that talks back to you

You won't do everything through a single agent. But one agent will be in charge of talking back to you.

Every push notification goes through it. Every escalation gets ranked by it. It's the one place that decides what's worth your attention. It's your representative to the AI world.

It has to be one agent because only the agent that knows all your priorities can decide when to interrupt you. Today every tool pings you on its own.

It hears about problems as they happen, and it checks on the things you care about without being asked. Cheap models like Luna can now run "is everything ok? anything new?" checks all day for almost nothing.

It runs in the cloud, so if one of your devices goes offline, it keeps going. It's your lifeline to your work.

# Sessions become tasks

You'll still have sessions that focus on one problem. But they get flexible. From inside a session you can spin off more sessions and reshape them as the work changes.

A session tracks a deliverable, not a conversation. So sessions naturally form a tree.

Sessions also stop being tied to one repo. Codex already did this, and Claude Code has had it for a while: one session can work across several repositories.

# The sidebar is the task tracker

![Our sidebar today](/thesis/sidebar.png)

This is our sidebar today. It's getting messy.

That the sidebar is the new task tracker is pretty much accepted by now. Some people think it goes away completely.

https://x.com/maria_rcks/status/2106620125923393849

We don't. We think it looks more like a modern vertical-tabs browser, like Arc: tasks, documents, previews and people mixed together in one list.

You might have a second sidebar for what other people are working on. That's where you plan what's next and explore the rest of the company.

And nobody has to hand-build it. It keeps a few simple building blocks we already have, and the agent generates the rest.

# Chat becomes a living document

Chat with agents is already changing. Codex, ChatGPT and T3 Code show visualizations inline instead of walls of text.

We think it goes further. Almost nobody scrolls up to reread old messages. So there's no reason for chat to be an append-only log. The history gets summarized, and the agent edits the content in place. The chat itself becomes an artifact.

The extreme version is one living document, one page tall. You give feedback, and the agent rewrites the sections that need to change. You shape the document directly instead of watching messages go by. We want to try that.

There are still settings you can flip by hand. But the real work happens with the agent: click anything it shows you, and it goes back to the agent. Muse does this well.

We're building all of this as a new app, not on top of the old Happy code.

# Why not just use Muse

We tried Muse and love the experience. Its hooks into Facebook and the rest of Meta's apps will be huge. The problem is the model. Muse Spark isn't a frontier model, and next to Opus 5.5, Fable or Astra it can't get the job done.

That's the main reason to use Happy. Bring the subscriptions you already pay for. When a better model comes out, switch to it without rebuilding your setup. dots only runs OpenAI. Muse only runs Meta. Happy runs whatever you pay for.

We'll host it for you, or you can run it yourself.

# The hard part: standards

The agent logs into your computers and your accounts. To get anything done, it has to hand some of that access to its sub-agents.

https://x.com/nikitabier/status/2107157904168239416

How much access should it hold? How does it pass that access down? How does a service know an agent is acting for you? How do you check what it did? One company can't settle this. It needs standards the whole industry agrees on.

https://x.com/btaylor/status/2107535182119244176

That's starting to happen. Meta and Sierra just announced the Personal Agent Protocol, an open standard for personal agents to identify themselves and prove they act for a real user. We'll build on standards like this rather than invent our own.

# How it spreads

Not through enterprise sales. Meta reached out about running an internal pilot of Happy because their engineers kept asking to use it at work. That's how good tools get into big companies: someone inside pushes for it.
