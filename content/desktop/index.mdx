# Welcome

A year ago most of us had one agent in one terminal. Now a team runs dozens, and a lot of the work starts as a message to one of them. More gets done. It also got harder to see what everyone else is working on, and to keep track of your own.

The way teams work is adjusting, and the tools with it: agent apps are growing team features, and team tools are growing agents. What a team is for hasn't changed, though. People form the mission, and the team and its agents exist to carry it out. In effect you are building one organism to do that, and we think it deserves a home of its own, with four things in it:

- **Your focus.** What needs you today, and nothing else. An agent asks you by name only when a decision is yours, and shared todo lists and calendars sit next to your tasks. What a calendar and a sidebar should be when agents do most of the work is something we are still working out.
- **Your agents.** Claude, Codex, Grok, and more in one window, on the subscriptions you already have. Tasks keep running when you close the app, and you can pick them up from your phone.
- **Alignment with your team.** A teammate's task appears in your sidebar when it starts, and what agents make is posted to a feed. You know where the mission stands without calling a meeting.
- **The team's always-on bots.** A task ends when the work is done. A bot keeps going and can own a whole area the way a person does: the release cycle, CI, research into where to distribute next. Everyone on the team asks the same one, and each bot gets only the keys its job needs.

![The full Happy Desktop window on a team server: the sidebar lists Activity, Artifacts, the Launch checklist, six bots and the team's tasks with their owners' faces, and Kirill's Voice waveform task is open, where he asks for an animated waveform and the agent says it is reading the voice bar components first.](/img/docs/welcome-window.webp "window chrome")

Happy is that home, built as a zero-dependency, open productivity stack. By that we mean a team should be able to do its work without stitching five other products together, and without depending on us. It is free and MIT licensed, it runs on your own computers and the ones your team connects, and the models run on the subscriptions you already pay for. You can read all of it, fork it, and host it yourself. How far along that is, and what comes next, is in [Vision](/vision/).

Three things are in the app. A **repository** is where your code is. A **task** is a piece of work you hand to an agent: one conversation, with a name and an owner. A **bot** is an agent that keeps a standing job. [Quick Start](/quick-start/) walks through all three.

**We use it to build it.** Happy is built by two people. Between 10 October and 14 November 2026 the two of us opened 419 tasks, and our agents worked in 491 separate checkouts across 16 repositories. The examples in these docs are ours.

## What does Happy cost?

Happy is free and MIT licensed, and the models run on your own Claude, Codex, and Grok subscriptions. Voice in the phone app is the only thing we charge for: 20 free minutes per 30 days, then up to 5 hours per 30 days on the in-app subscription. With your own ElevenLabs agent there is no limit from Happy. See [Voice](/voice/).
